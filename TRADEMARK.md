# Trademark policy

The **code** in this repository is free. The **name**, the **logo**, and the status of being
the **official** project are not.

This distinction is deliberate, and this document explains it plainly so that nobody has to
guess where the line is.

## What you may do, freely and without asking

Everything the licences allow, which is a great deal:

- Build the firmware, flash it, modify it, and run it on as many devices as you like.
- Run, modify and self-host the reference relay, for yourself, your family, your employer,
  or your homelab.
- Print, modify, and sell the case.
- Fork the whole project.
- Sell hardware kits built from this design.
- Write about the project, make videos about it, teach with it, and quote it.

The firmware, the case and the tools are MIT licensed. The reference relay is AGPL-3.0. See
[LICENSE-MIT](LICENSE-MIT) and [LICENSE-AGPL](LICENSE-AGPL).

## What the trademark covers

The name **Remote Wake**, the Remote Wake logo, and the `remotewake.com` domain are
trademarks of Puresoft Ltd. Trademark law is separate from copyright law: a permissive
software licence grants rights to the *code*, never to the *brand*.

So, specifically — you may **not**, without written permission:

- Name your fork, product, service or company "Remote Wake", or anything close enough to be
  confusing.
- Use the Remote Wake logo as the logo of your fork, product or service.
- Describe your fork or service as "official", "certified", "endorsed by", or "the" Remote
  Wake — or imply any of that through styling, domain names or app-store listings.
- Register domain names, social accounts, or app-store listings using the mark.
- Sell hardware branded as "Remote Wake" or in packaging that presents itself as ours.

## What you may say about your fork

Accurate, descriptive references are fine and always have been. You do not need permission to
write any of these:

- "Compatible with the Remote Wake protocol."
- "Based on Remote Wake."
- "A fork of Remote Wake."
- "Works with Remote Wake dongles."

The test is whether a reasonable person would think your thing *is* ours, or is endorsed by
us. Describing a relationship is fine; implying identity or endorsement is not.

## Why the mark exists

Because the code is genuinely free, and we intend to keep it that way.

An open-source hardware project needs *something* that a fork cannot simply take, or the work
of building it — and of supporting the people who use it — has no way to pay for itself. For
this project that something is the trademark and the hosted service, not the code. Nothing in
the firmware is crippled, there is no "community edition", and self-hosting is a supported,
documented, first-class path with its own reference implementation in this repository.

That boundary is not going to move. Holding the mark is what lets us promise that.
